## Market Signal Brief

Source: business-brief.md (FuturIQ — coaches/consultants/creators/service providers, overwhelmed by AI tools, want to know "what system to build first").

### Demand Pattern

**Public evidence (from web search, Sept 2026):**
- Claude Code's "Agent Teams" / multi-agent / subagent orchestration feature is a live, actively-covered topic right now — multiple 2026 videos and blog posts exist specifically on "Claude Code Agent Teams," "subagents," "skills & handoffs," "multi-agent workflows." This confirms the underlying capability is real, recent, and already generating creator interest.
- Separately, there is a distinct, high-volume wave of "AI agents for business/money" content — "How to Make Money as an AI Consultant," "AI Agents Masterclass: Build & Sell," "AI Automation Agency" videos — signaling strong audience appetite for AI-agents-as-revenue-system framing.
- One search summary cited the AI-coaching tools market "growing 20%+ annually" and a Gartner-style claim about enterprise agent adoption by end of 2026 — third-party claims surfaced in search results, not independently verified; treat as soft, unconfirmed context, not hard data.
- All the Claude Code / agent-team content found is written for **developers** (terminal-first, "subagent," "orchestration," "handoffs" vocabulary). The searches did not surface existing content that translates "Claude Code agent team" into **business-builder** language (positioning, offer, lead magnet, follow-up sequence) for a non-technical coach/consultant audience.

**Inference (not directly evidenced):** This developer-vs-business-audience gap is a plausible whitespace — a technically real, currently-trending capability with almost no content bridging it to FuturIQ's actual audience. Absence of a hit in a handful of searches isn't proof of absence, but the pattern across three separate searches is consistent enough to treat as a real signal.

### Audience Psychology

- **Wants:** A single, credible "starting system" so they can stop tool-hopping and finally point AI at revenue instead of novelty. They want to feel ahead of their competitors, not behind.
- **Fears:** That they're already behind on AI, that whatever they build will look and sound like everyone else's AI-generated content, and that "agents" is just more complexity stacked on the tool overwhelm they already have.
- **Misunderstands:** Conflates "using more AI tools" with "having an AI strategy." Believes the blocker is not knowing which tool to use, when the real blocker is not having a system that turns one idea into a full go-to-market pipeline.
- **Secretly hopes:** That one afternoon and one well-built "agent team" can do the strategic work they'd otherwise pay a consultant or agency thousands for — and that it's simple enough for a non-coder to run.

### Risk

1. It plays as a raw terminal screen-recording with dev vocabulary and no plain-English translation.
2. All five outputs are shown at equal, shallow depth — five generic prompts wearing an "agent team" costume.
3. No real proof point (an actual resulting offer, a plausible price tag, a believable before/after).
4. It blends into the saturated "$10k/month AI agency" hype genre.

### Strategic Recommendation

- **Narrow the concept:** Hero demo = "one business idea → positioning + a sellable offer." Compress YouTube angles, lead magnet, and follow-up into a fast "this same team also outputs..." sequence in the back third.
- **Target advanced-beginner / practitioner.** Avoid jargon (subagent, orchestration, handoff); re-narrate every step in business terms ("this is your strategist").
- **Lead with the money-in-systems thesis before showing any screen.**
- **Use the offer as the on-camera proof point** — a specific, priced, sellable offer coming out of the system.
- **Differentiate explicitly from the "AI agency/get rich" wave** — e.g., "this isn't an agency in a box, it's a positioning system."
