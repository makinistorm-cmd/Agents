## Market Signal Brief (brief-only)

**Labels used on every claim:**
- **[Brief]**: stated in `business-brief.md`
- **[Inference]**: the analyst's reasoning, with a "because"
- **[Unknown]**: needs real evidence, which is named

---

### What the brief actually establishes (only [Brief] items)
- **[Brief]** Creator: FuturIQ.
- **[Brief]** Audience: "Coaches, consultants, creators, service providers, and entrepreneurs who know AI is important but are overwhelmed by tools and do not know what business system to build first."
- **[Brief]** Core promise: "Help entrepreneurs build AI-powered business systems that create leverage, leads, and revenue."
- **[Brief]** Video goal: a sub-15-minute YouTube video that feels "practical, advanced, and immediately useful."
- **[Brief]** Video concept: "Build a Claude Code agent team that turns one business idea into positioning, an offer, YouTube angles, a lead magnet, and a follow-up sequence."
- **[Brief]** Offer path: the video should "naturally lead to" a downloadable Revenue Agent template, a workshop, or a paid AI systems offer.
- **[Brief]** Voice: "Direct, strategic, high-energy, premium, practical, no fluff."
- **[Brief]** Constraints: not a generic AI tools tutorial; not a basic prompt engineering lesson; show the AI-workflow-to-revenue connection; "The money is not in tools. The money is in systems."; beginner-friendly "without making it basic."
- **[Brief]** The brief contains **no** search data, audience research, past video performance, competitor information, or proof of results.

---

### Demand Pattern
- **[Brief]** The only demand signal is a described state: knows AI matters, overwhelmed by tools, unsure what to build first. There are no numbers, queries, comments, or sales behind it.
- **[Inference]** The pain is "I don't know what to build first," not "I can't earn enough," *because that's how the brief words it*. No income problem is named.
- **[Inference]** "The money is in systems" is the creator's hypothesis about the audience's mistake, *because it's written as a correction, not as an observed behavior*.
- **[Unknown]** Whether anyone searches for this. *Evidence: YouTube and Google autocomplete, and the channel's search-traffic report.*
- **[Unknown]** Whether the overwhelm framing outperforms an outcome framing ("more clients"). *Evidence: past video analytics or a title test.*

### Audience Psychology
- **[Brief]** They want leverage, leads, and revenue. They fear falling behind on AI. They feel overwhelmed.
- **[Inference]** They misunderstand AI as a tool-selection problem, *because the brief's "money is in systems" line is written as a correction*.
- **[Inference]** They secretly hope one setup replaces the decisions they're avoiding, *because "turns one business idea into" five assets promises exactly that*.
- **[Inference]** Sophistication is mixed to skeptical, *because the brief says "beginner-friendly" and "advanced," and a premium voice implies they've seen hype before*.
- **[Unknown]** How skeptical they are and what they've tried. *Evidence: past comments, or 5 short interviews.*

---

### Assessment 1: is "booked, not broke 1:1 experts" supported by the brief?
**Verdict: it's an inference that narrows and departs from the brief. It isn't a restatement.**
- **[Brief]** The brief names five groups. Coaches, consultants, and service providers overlap with the proposed buyer. Creators and general entrepreneurs don't.
- **[Brief]** The brief never mentions 1:1 delivery, being booked, referrals, discovery calls, or "not broke."
- **[Inference]** Narrowing is a legitimate strategy *choice*. It should be presented as one, not as what the brief says.
- **[Inference]** **Pain tension:** a booked expert already has demand, *so they may feel less of the "unsure where to start" confusion the brief describes*. Their problem is capacity, which is a different problem.
- **[Inference]** **"Leads" tension:** someone with referrals may not feel a lead problem, *and the brief's outputs (YouTube angles, lead magnet, follow-up) are top-of-funnel assets that fit someone short on leads better than someone short on hours*.
- **[Unknown]** Whether this subset is large and responsive on this channel. *Evidence: an "how do you earn?" poll, plus who comments and buys.*

### Assessment 2: is "income capped by the calendar" the core pain in the brief?
**Verdict: it's inferred. It isn't in the brief.**
- **[Brief]** The closest stated idea is "leverage."
- **[Inference]** "Leverage" *can* mean escaping time-for-money, *because that's a common meaning of the word*. The brief doesn't say so.
- **[Inference]** It's a sharper, more emotional pain that may click better, but it remains the team's hypothesis.
- **[Unknown]** Whether the audience describes their problem this way. *Evidence: ask 5 people, free text, "what's the biggest thing limiting your income right now?"*

### Risk
- **[Brief]** No generic tools tutorial. No basic prompt lesson.
- **[Inference]** The control beat could drift into a *prompt comparison*, close to the brief's "not basic prompt engineering" line, *because its frame is prompt vs. system*.
- **[Inference]** If the Relay ties or loses, the proof undermines the hook. If it wins by a lot, a skeptic may suspect the prompt was weak, *because "fair" rests on the creator's own scoring*.
- **[Inference]** **"Validate before you price" is the likeliest unbelievable moment.** An AI teammate can't truly validate demand without real data. The brief never mentions validation.
- **[Inference]** "Stop Selling Hours" + "AI team" may read as "AI replaces your work" unless the output is shown being *used*, not just generated.
- **[Unknown]** Whether the 4-teammate naming helps or hurts. *Evidence: 5 people from the target group explain it back.*

### Strategic Recommendation
- **[Inference]** Treat the 1:1 narrowing as a **hypothesis to test**, not a given.
- **[Inference]** Keep the brief's pain (overwhelm, "what do I build first") as the entry and test "calendar cap" as the hook. Both fit together: *"you don't need more tools, you need one revenue system."*
- **[Inference]** Keep the control beat only if it's cheap and the scoring is shown on screen. Frame it as "single prompt vs. system," not "ChatGPT vs. Claude."
- **[Inference]** **Cut or soften "validate"** unless the video shows a real validation action (a test post, real replies).
- **[Brief]** End on the Revenue Agent template and show the revenue link. Whether the template, workshop, or paid offer is the primary ask is [Unknown].

### Evidence to Collect Before Recording
1. **Audience poll (same day):** "What caps your income most? A) hours in my calendar B) not enough leads C) too many AI tools, no plan D) other." This tests the pain *and* who's actually in the audience.
2. **Own analytics (free):** top past titles and search terms. Do "systems" or "agent" titles beat tool titles?
3. **5 people, 2 questions:** "What limits your income right now?" and "What have you tried with AI?" Count time vs. leads vs. tools.
4. **Control-beat dry run:** run the comparison 2–3 times on different ideas and log the 3 checks.
5. **3-second title test:** show the title to 5 target viewers and ask what it's about and who it's for.
