## Unique Mechanism — The Revenue Relay

| Teammate | Job |
|---|---|
| **Scout** | Tests whether there's real demand, before anyone writes a word |
| **Strategist** | Turns validated demand into one priced, sellable offer |
| **Publisher** | Turns that offer into content that points at the sale |
| **Closer** | Builds the lead magnet and follow-up that turn attention into buyers |

**The insight:**
> Validate before you price. Price before you sell. Do it all in one pass and you're decorating a guess.

**Why this beats "productize your service" advice, not just a single prompt:** the productization content this niche has already consumed tells the reader to package expertise into a fixed offer. It does not tell them which piece of their expertise the market will actually pay a fixed price for. A single AI pass has the same blind spot — it writes a plausible offer because it's good at plausible, not because anything confirmed demand for it. The Relay's separation is the whole point: Scout is not allowed to pitch, and Strategist is not allowed to invent findings Scout didn't produce. The gap between "here's advice" and "here's a validated offer" is the gap the video sells.

### The Control Beat, Redesigned (fixes the credibility risk)

**The risk, stated plainly:** this audience uses ChatGPT daily. If the control prompt on screen is a lazy one-liner, they will clock it as a strawman in the first ten seconds and the whole believability case collapses.

**The fix: make the control prompt the best single prompt a smart, ChatGPT-fluent, "I already read the productize post" user would actually write.**

Not this (too weak, reads as rigged):
> "Write me an offer for a coach who does discovery calls."

This instead (what a sophisticated viewer would actually type, because it already encodes the advice this niche has absorbed):
> "I'm a coach doing free discovery calls. Referrals keep me busy but everything I sell is 1:1 and priced by the hour. Help me productize this into a fixed-price offer with tiers, a name, and pricing that isn't tied to my time. Make it sound premium and specific to my niche, not generic."

That prompt is genuinely good. It's the prompt someone gets after reading the Forbes piece. Winning against it is the actual proof. Winning against a lazy prompt is not.

**What the Relay output must show that this strong control cannot, checked against three specific, nameable criteria:**

1. **Real demand language.** A phrase, fear, or objection Scout actually found — the words a real buyer uses, not a category ("coaches want more clients"). The control can only generalize; it has no research pass, so it cannot produce a specific line a real prospect would say.
2. **A pricing structure tied to a named deliverable or outcome, not a generic tier label.** A strong control prompt can also say "tiered, not hourly" — so the differentiator isn't "not hourly," it's that the Relay's price attaches to something concrete the Scout pass surfaced (a specific outcome, timeframe, or deliverable the market already cares about), not a template tier ("Starter / Pro / VIP").
3. **A named qualifying filter — who this is explicitly *not* for.** Drawn from something Scout found (a misunderstanding, a bad-fit pattern, a wrong expectation), not a generic exclusion ("not for beginners"). A prompt with no research pass cannot produce this; it can only guess an audience, never exclude one on evidence.

**The go/no-go test — run before filming, not during:**

1. Run the strong control prompt three times. Keep the best of the three. Do not cherry-pick a bad run.
2. Run the full Relay once on the same idea, unedited on the first pass.
3. Score the Relay output against the three criteria above. Each one is binary: present with a specific, checkable detail, or not.
4. **Go** if the Relay clearly wins on at least 2 of 3.
5. **No-go** if the Relay wins on 0 or 1: don't film this demo idea. Either pick a harder/more specific demo business, or send Scout back with a narrower brief before re-testing. Never edit the control output to make it weaker, and never edit the Relay output to make the gap look wider than it was on the actual run.

This is the same discipline the market-signal brief calls for ("no strawman," "hard go/no-go gate") applied as a concrete, repeatable checklist instead of a general warning.

8. **The real, unedited build timer**, frozen at the end.
9. **The template folder, opened live**, during the CTA — proof the free asset exists and matches what's on screen.
