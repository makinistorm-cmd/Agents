# Content Strategy — "Stop Selling Hours" (v3)

> Built on `01-market-signal-brief.md` (29/35) and `02-offer-architecture.md`. Audience: booked-not-broke 1:1 experts, intermediate-to-skeptical, daily ChatGPT users. Findings are shown at full resolution on screen — nothing simplified for this crowd.

## Content Strategy

### Best Title

**"Stop Selling Hours. Build This AI Team Instead."**

**Title re-test against the saturation finding:** the market-signal brief flags that "stop trading hours for dollars" is an established, already-monetized phrase (Forbes, a paid roadmap product, an existing YouTube video). That's a real risk for *originality*, but it's not a reason to drop the phrase — it's evidence the phrase already converts attention for this exact buyer. The risk isn't the first four words, it's the back half. "Build this AI team instead" is what keeps the title from reading as one more productization listicle: it promises a build the viewer can watch happen, not another argument for why they should productize. That promise is also what the video's actual positioning is about — the gap isn't "should you build an offer," it's "how do you know what to build instead of guessing." Tested two alternatives:
- *"Your Calendar Is Not a Business Model"* — fresher, zero prior-art collision, but fails the 3-second test: it reads as a mindset line, not a video with a mechanism. Weaker click.
- *"You've Heard 'Productize Your Service.' Here's What They Skipped."* — names the gap precisely, matches the hook, but is a sentence, not a title; slower to parse, and "here's what they skipped" undersells the build.

Verdict: **keep it.** The saturated phrase does the clicking; "build this AI team instead" does the differentiating. Cut "Claude Code" from title and thumbnail — kept off both per the coordinator's constraint.

### The Traceability Beat (8:05–8:35, exactly 30 seconds)

This is the fix for the risk that an LLM can invent plausible-sounding "demand language." The split screen must prove each Strategist line came from an actual Scout line, not from the model's own confidence.

**Layout:** left pane = Scout's raw findings (scrollable, visible text). Right pane = Strategist's offer draft. Three on-screen labels appear one at a time, ~9–10 seconds each:

1. **0:00–0:10 — "Demand language."** Highlight (yellow) the specific Scout finding — the real buyer phrase or objection. Animated connector line draws from that highlighted text to the matching phrase in the Strategist's offer copy. Caption: *"Scout finding → offer language."*
2. **0:10–0:20 — "Outcome-tied price."** Highlight the Scout line naming the concrete outcome/deliverable/timeframe the market cares about. Connector draws to the price line in the offer (not a generic "Starter/Pro/VIP" tier — the specific number tied to that outcome). Caption: *"Scout finding → the price."*
3. **0:20–0:30 — "Qualifying filter."** Highlight the Scout line describing a bad-fit pattern or wrong expectation. Connector draws to the "this isn't for you if..." line in the offer. Caption: *"Scout finding → who this excludes."*

Each label is binary and checkable on screen — the audience isn't asked to trust a vibe, they're shown the exact sentence the claim came from.

### Screen Recording Beats

Exact moments to zoom, cut, or show output, in order:

1. **1:50–2:15** — Zoom on the cursor as the strong control prompt is typed live, full sentence visible, captioned "This is a genuinely good prompt."
2. **2:15–2:20** — Hard cut to full-screen control output, held 5 full seconds, no voiceover during the hold.
3. **2:50–6:30** — As each Scout finding populates, zoom in and underline the specific phrase before narrating it. Do this for each of the 2–3 findings called out — don't summarize past the literal text.
4. **7:10–7:40** — Split-second before/after cut showing the Strategist's first draft line next to the revised line (a visible diff, not a dissolve).
5. **7:40–8:05** — Hard cut to the priced offer, full screen, largest text on screen in the whole video. No lower-third. Let it sit in silence.
6. **8:05–8:35** — Split screen assembles: left pane scrolls to the exact Scout line, highlight animation, connector line draws to the right pane, label pops in. Repeat 3x per the plan above. Keep it a rhythmic, repeatable visual pattern so it reads as a checklist, not decoration.
7. **8:35–9:50** — Full split screen: control output vs. Relay offer, both visible simultaneously, so the size/specificity difference is a single glance.
8. **10:10–12:00** — Montage cuts between Publisher's content angle screen and Closer's lead-magnet/email screen, each held long enough to actually read, not a blur.
9. **12:00** — Freeze-frame on the build timer the instant Closer's output appears. Hold the freeze into the recap.
10. **13:20–14:00** — Live screen capture of the template folder opening, files visible by name (Control Prompt Card, four teammate files, runbook, side-by-side sheet) — proof the free asset matches what was shown.
