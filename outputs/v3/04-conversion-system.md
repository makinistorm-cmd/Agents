## Lead Magnet

### Revenue Agent Template
*Run the same fair test on your own idea — the strong prompt, the Relay, and the exact scorecard from the video.*

**Contents**

1. **Strong Control Prompt Card.** The identical multi-sentence prompt typed on screen — not a lazy one-liner, the one a smart, ChatGPT-fluent, "I already read the productize post" user would write. Instructions: run it three times on your own idea, keep the best of three, don't cherry-pick a weak run.
2. **Four teammate files:** Scout, Strategist, Publisher, Closer. Job titles, not code. No developer language anywhere in the files.
3. **Business Brief Starter.** Audience, current income model (hourly, retainer, referral), constraints, voice. Five minutes.
4. **Runbook, "Run Your Own Relay in 20 Minutes."** The literal copy-paste handoffs between teammates. Default version runs in a plain chat window. No install required.
5. **3-Criteria Side-by-Side Scorecard.** The exact checklist from the video, built to score your own two outputs:

   | Criterion | Present in control? | Present in Relay? | Which exact line proves it |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Demand language (a real buyer phrase or objection, not a category) | | | |
   | Outcome-tied price (attached to a named deliverable or outcome, not a generic tier label) | | | |
   | Qualifying filter ("who this is not for," drawn from an actual finding) | | | |

   **The honesty rule, printed at the bottom of the sheet:** *If the Relay doesn't beat your control prompt on at least 2 of 3, don't force a win. Tighten your Brief and rerun Scout before you retest. A strong prompt that already ties for your idea is information — it usually means your brief needs one more sentence of specificity, not that the system failed.*

**Why someone wants it now:** they just watched a genuinely strong prompt lose on a checklist they can verify themselves, not on a vibe. The template lets them run that identical, specific test on the one idea they actually care about — their own — in the next twenty minutes, instead of taking the video's word for it.

**First-20-minutes quick win**
- Strong control prompt, 3 runs, keep the best (4 min)
- Brief Starter (5 min)
- Scout (7 min)
- Strategist (4 min)
- Score both outputs on the 3-criteria sheet — this is the win: a scored answer, not a guess, on your own business.

## Friction Check

- **The Scorecard turns proof into homework.** Three checkable criteria is more rigorous than "look how much better this sounds," but it's also more effort than skimming a PDF. If the on-video scorecard beat (8:05–8:35) isn't fast and visual, the template's version reads as new work invented after the fact, not "the same thing you just watched." Keep the video beat crisp so the template feels like a rerun, not a homework assignment.
- **Email 1's CTA depends entirely on completion.** "Reply with your score" only works if the reader actually ran both prompts and filled the sheet. Anyone who just skims the template has nothing to reply with — expect a real drop-off between download and reply, and don't read silence as disengagement from the whole list.
- **The honest failure case needs to land right, or it reads as the method failing.** Since the control prompt is now genuinely strong, some viewers' first runs will tie or lose to it. Email 3 has to normalize this immediately (tighten the Brief, rerun Scout) or a non-win reads as "this doesn't actually work," not as the intended go/no-go signal the offer-architecture doc built in.
- **Placeholders are load-bearing, not decorative.** Email 3's `[insert exact offer sentence from the recording]` and Email 4's `[generic line]` / `[real offer line]` must be filled from real, run-for-real output before send. If they ship with a placeholder or a written-not-run example, the fair-test credibility the whole system is built on breaks the moment a sharp reader notices.
- **"You've already heard this advice" can boomerang.** If Email 2 lingers on "this isn't new" without pivoting fast to the sequencing mechanism (validate → price → sell), a skeptical reader concludes there's nothing here worth their time. The pivot sentence has to arrive in the same email, not a later one.
- **Office hours makes a live-reply promise.** "I read these" and "I'll score it live" require an actual process on the other end. Same risk as the prior version, unchanged: don't send this email until the reply and office-hours logistics are real.
- **"Score" language risks a grading feel.** Used often across the emails, it needs to stay checklist-plain ("present or not, which line proves it"), not gamified or exam-like — especially in the Sprint email, where "pressure-tested against the Scorecard" must read as help, not a pass/fail test of the reader's business idea.
- **No paid ask before Email 6.** Unchanged from the prior version and still correct — office hours and the template stay free, the Sprint is the first dollar figure the reader sees.
