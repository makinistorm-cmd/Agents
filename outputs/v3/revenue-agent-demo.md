## 1. Executive Summary

**[Brief]** FuturIQ's audience knows AI matters, is overwhelmed by tools, and doesn't know what business system to build first. The promise is AI systems that create leverage, leads, and revenue.

**[Inference]** The sharpest buyer inside that audience is the booked 1:1 expert whose income is capped by their calendar. The brief doesn't say this. It's a strategy choice the team made, and it needs testing (§13). **[Inference]** For that buyer, the gap isn't *knowing you should productize*. It's **knowing what to productize before you guess.**

FuturIQ's video runs a fair test on screen. First, the strongest single prompt a smart ChatGPT user would write. Then the **Revenue Relay**, four AI teammates that research the buyer, then price the offer, then build what sells it. The relay has to win on three checks the viewer can verify, and each check is traced back to the research line it came from.

Viewers download the same test as the **Revenue Agent Template**. Seven emails over 14 days move them through free office hours to the **$497 Offer Architecture Sprint**, and qualified businesses on to the **Revenue Systems Build**.

**Market score (brief-only): 22/35.** These are judgment calls, not measured data. The earlier web-research run scored 29. The 7-point gap is roughly what outside evidence was adding. The weakest category is searchability (2/5), followed by urgency, clickability, credibility, and runtime (3/5 each). Five cheap checks in §13 would turn the biggest inferences into evidence.

## 5. The Mechanism — The Revenue Relay

**Scout** researches the buyer → **Strategist** prices the offer → **Publisher** builds content that points at the sale → **Closer** builds the lead magnet and follow-up.

> **Research before you price. Price before you sell. Do it all in one pass and you're decorating a guess.**

**Why "research," not "validate" [Inference]:** an AI teammate can't truly *validate* demand. Without real data, Scout's findings are informed hypotheses. Claiming validation would be the video's least believable moment. **The fix:** feed Scout real inputs, such as poll answers, client quotes, or DM language (§13), and say on camera that real buyer conversations are the final test.

A single pass writes toward the sentence it already wants and bends the evidence to fit. In the Relay, Scout isn't allowed to pitch, and Strategist can only use what Scout actually found. **What's new here isn't the advice. It's that the order is enforced.**

## 6. The Fair Test (the credibility fix)

**The control prompt** is the strong one a smart viewer would actually write:
> "I'm a coach doing free discovery calls. Referrals keep me busy but everything I sell is 1:1 and priced by the hour. Help me productize this into a fixed-price offer with tiers, a name, and pricing that isn't tied to my time. Make it sound premium and specific to my niche, not generic."

**The Relay must win on three checks, and each one is traced to a Scout finding:**
1. **Demand language:** a buyer phrase or objection from Scout's findings, not a category. **[Inference]** Scout working from a brief alone produces *plausible* buyer language, not *real* buyer language. This check is only strong if Scout is fed real words: poll answers, client quotes, DMs (§13). Say on screen which one you used.
2. **Outcome-tied price:** a price attached to a named outcome or deliverable, not "Starter / Pro / VIP."
3. **Qualifying filter:** who this is *not* for, drawn from a real Scout finding.

**Go/no-go test (run before filming):**
- Run the control 3 times and keep the best.
- Run the Relay once, unedited.
- Score each check yes or no.
- **Go at 2 of 3 or better.**
- At 0–1, don't film this idea. Pick a harder one or narrow the Scout brief.
- **[Unknown]** Whether the Relay wins at all. Nothing in the brief can predict it. Only the dry run can (§13 #4).
- Never edit either output to widen the gap.
