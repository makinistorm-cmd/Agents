## 1. Executive Summary

"Stop trading hours for dollars" is a proven, paid-for pain. People already buy roadmaps, read Forbes columns, and watch YouTube videos about it. The advice is everywhere. So the gap isn't *knowing you should productize*. The gap is **knowing what to productize before you guess.**

FuturIQ's video runs a fair test on screen. First, the strongest single prompt a smart ChatGPT user would write. Then the **Revenue Relay**, four AI teammates that validate demand, then price it, then build what sells it. The relay has to win on three checks the viewer can verify, and each check is traced back to the research line it came from.

Viewers download the same test as the **Revenue Agent Template**. Seven emails over 14 days move them through free office hours to the **$497 Offer Architecture Sprint**, and qualified businesses on to the **Revenue Systems Build**.

**Market score: 29/35** (the v1 concept scored 28). The weakest category is credibility, at 3/5, and it's fixed by the go/no-go test in §6, not by stronger claims.

## 5. The Mechanism — The Revenue Relay

**Scout** checks demand → **Strategist** prices it → **Publisher** builds content that points at the sale → **Closer** builds the lead magnet and follow-up.

> **Validate before you price. Price before you sell. Do it all in one pass and you're decorating a guess.**

A single pass writes toward the sentence it already wants and bends the evidence to fit. In the Relay, Scout isn't allowed to pitch, and Strategist can only use what Scout actually found. **What's new here isn't the advice. It's that the order is enforced.**

## 6. The Fair Test (the credibility fix)

**The control prompt** is the strong one a smart viewer would actually write:
> "I'm a coach doing free discovery calls. Referrals keep me busy but everything I sell is 1:1 and priced by the hour. Help me productize this into a fixed-price offer with tiers, a name, and pricing that isn't tied to my time. Make it sound premium and specific to my niche, not generic."

**The Relay must win on three checks, and each one is traced to a Scout finding:**
1. **Demand language:** a real buyer phrase or objection that Scout found, not a category.
2. **Outcome-tied price:** a price attached to a named outcome or deliverable, not "Starter / Pro / VIP."
3. **Qualifying filter:** who this is *not* for, drawn from a real Scout finding.

**Go/no-go test (run before filming):**
- Run the control 3 times and keep the best.
- Run the Relay once, unedited.
- Score each check yes or no.
- **Go at 2 of 3 or better.**
- At 0–1, don't film this idea. Pick a harder one or narrow the Scout brief.
- Never edit either output to widen the gap.

## 8. Lead Magnet — Revenue Agent Template

*Run the same fair test on your own idea.*

1. **Strong Control Prompt Card:** the exact prompt from the video. Run it 3 times and keep the best.
2. **Four teammate files:** Scout, Strategist, Publisher, Closer. No developer language anywhere.
3. **Business Brief Starter:** takes 5 minutes.
4. **Runbook, "Run Your Own Relay in 20 Minutes":** plain chat window by default, nothing to install.
5. **3-Check Scorecard:** columns for control, Relay, and *which exact line proves it*.
   **Honesty rule, printed on the sheet:** *if the Relay doesn't beat your best prompt on 2 of 3, tighten your brief and rerun Scout. A tie is information, not failure.*

**The first 20 minutes:**
- Control ×3 (4 min)
- Brief (5 min)
- Scout (7 min)
- Strategist (4 min)
- Score both outputs.

- The priced offer in silence
- The traceability beat

- Tool tours
- Developer vocabulary on camera
- "Save time" or "10x" lines
- Income claims
- Prompt-writing tips
- Speaking to creators in the hook
- Equal time per teammate
- A premium pitch in the video
- "14-minute build"
